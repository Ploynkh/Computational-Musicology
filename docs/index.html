Background & Corpus

Research Question

Can R&B still make you move, or does it now aim to leave you speechless?

This project examines how R&B has evolved from the 1990s to the 2020s, not just in sound, but in purpose. It asks whether a genre once defined by groove, rhythm, and dancefloor energy has shifted toward atmosphere, introspection, and personal emotional experience.

The Corpus

The corpus consists of 50 tracks, with 25 from each era, selected to represent two distinct stylistic poles of R&B.

The 1990s selection draws from artists such as Janet Jackson, Aaliyah, Blackstreet, Usher, and Mariah Carey. These tracks were chosen for their strong rhythmic structure, high danceability, and cultural association with social listening environments such as clubs, parties, and collective movement. They represent a period in which R&B was closely tied to the body and the dancefloor, built around groove, energy, and outward emotional expression.

The 2020s selection features artists such as SZA, Frank Ocean, Ariana Grande, and Summer Walker. These tracks were selected for their slower tempos, softer production textures, and emphasis on mood and emotional nuance. They represent a contemporary R&B more likely to be experienced in personal, intimate contexts such as late-night drives, quiet evenings, or moments of private reflection.

Approach

Using Spotify’s audio features and audio analysis tools, this dashboard investigates whether the stylistic differences between the two eras can be measured quantitatively. Four analytical dimensions are examined across the pages of this dashboard.

Track-level features look at danceability, energy, valence, and loudness to capture the most immediate and measurable qualities of each track.

Chroma features examine harmonic content and pitch class profiles to reveal how the tonal character of R&B has shifted across eras.

Timbral features analyse sound texture, density, and instrumentation to capture how the sonic palette of the genre has changed.

Temporal features focus on tempo, rhythm, and structural patterns to determine whether the rhythmic drive of R&B has weakened over time.

By comparing these dimensions across both corpora, the project aims to determine whether R&B has shifted from music designed for movement to music designed for mood.
